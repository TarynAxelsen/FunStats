Review Questions: Module 4

Once you have watched the lecture recordings you should be able to answer the following Threshold questions (solutions can be found at the end of this Workbook).

Write your answers in the boxes below. Tick the checkbox if you believe your answer is correct. Click Show model answers to compare.

(i) What statistical method is used to quantify the relationship between two quantitative variables?
(ii) What graph is used to display the relationship between two quantitative variables?
(iii) What is the difference between an explanatory variable and a response variable? Give an example.
(iv) List the four features used to describe a scatterplot.
(v) What is the general form of the equation of the regression line?
(vi) Why is caution needed when extrapolating?
Model answers

(i) To measure the strength of a relationship between two quantitative variables, we use correlation.

(ii) A scatterplot.

(iii) A response (dependent) variable measures an outcome in the study. An explanatory (independent) variable explains or influences changes in the response variable. For example, in a study of the age and height of children, "age" would be the explanatory variable and "height" would be the response variable, since height depends on age (rather than age depending on height).

(iv) Form (e.g. linear, non-linear/curved), Direction (positive or negative), Scatter (small/medium/large), and Outliers.

(v) y = b0 + b1x, where y is the predicted value of the response variable, b0 is the y-intercept, b1 is the slope of the regression line, and x is the explanatory variable.

(vi) Because we can't be sure that the linear trend of the data continues beyond the values that we have.
