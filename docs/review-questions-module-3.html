Review Questions: Module 3

Once you have watched the lecture recordings you should be able to answer the following Threshold questions (solutions can be found at the end of this Workbook).

Write your answers in the boxes below. Tick the checkbox if you believe your answer is correct. Click Show model answers to compare.

(i) Why is the normal model so important in statistics?
(ii) Which two values are needed to specify a particular normal model?
(iii) What do areas under normal curves represent?
(iv) What is meant by standardising? How is data standardised? What notation is commonly used for standardised data?
(v) What is the purpose of standardising?
(vi) In the normal model, what percentage of values falls within one standard deviation of the mean? Within two standard deviations of the mean? Within three standard deviations of the mean?
Model answers

(i) Many naturally occurring quantitative variables follow an approximately symmetric distribution, so the normal model is a useful approximation to reality.

(ii) Mean μ (measure of centre) and standard deviation σ (measure of spread).

(iii) The areas represent theoretical percentages of the population, or probabilities.

(iv) Standardising means converting a score to a Z score (the number of standard deviations from the mean). Data is standardised using the formula Z = (y - μ) / σ, where y = the score of interest, μ = the population mean, and σ = the population standard deviation. Standardised data is represented by Z.

(v) It allows the probability of a score occurring within our normal distribution to be calculated. It also allows the comparison of two scores that are from different normal distributions.

(vi) 68% of values lie within one standard deviation either side of the mean. 95% of values lie within two standard deviations either side of the mean. 99.7% of values lie within three standard deviations either side of the mean.
