3.4 Question 1: Using Standard Normal Tables

Draw a picture of the standard Normal curve. Mark in the position and values of the mean and standard deviation. A standard Normal model has a mean \(\mu = 0\) and standard deviation \(\sigma = 1\).

Now use your diagram of the Normal Distribution and Table Z to answer the following questions.

(a) What percentage of a standard Normal model is found in each region?

  1. \(z > 1.5\)
  2. \(z < 2.3\)
  3. \(z > -1.72\)
  4. \(z < -0.56\)
  5. *\(-2 < z < 1.34\)
  6. *\(2.3 < z < 3.1\)

Try it yourself. Use the explorer below to shade each region and read off the proportion. Enter the z-value(s) from each part above and check your hand-table answer against the live calculation.

Standard Normal model: mean = 0, standard deviation = 1
and

i. z>1.5: from Table Z, area = 0.9332. Required area = 1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668, i.e. 6.68%.

ii. z<2.3: from Table Z, area = 0.9893, i.e. 98.93%.

iii. z>-1.72: from Table Z, area = 0.0427. Required area = 1 - 0.0427 = 0.9573, i.e. 95.73%.

iv. z<-0.56: from Table Z, area = 0.2877, i.e. 28.77%.

v. Betweeen -2 and 1.34: area at z=-2 is 0.0228; area at z=1.34 is 0.9099. Required area = 0.9099 - 0.0228 = 0.8871, i.e. 88.71%.

vi. Between 2.3 and 3.1: area at z=2.3 is 0.9893; area at z=3.1 is 0.9990. Required area = 0.9990 - 0.9893 = 0.0097, i.e. 0.97%.

(b) *In a standard Normal model, what value(s) of z cut(s) off the region described?

First step: always draw a picture!

  1. *the highest 10%
  2. *the highest 60%
  3. *the lowest 2%
  4. *the lowest 40%
  5. *the middle 95%
  6. *the middle 50%

Try it yourself. Switch the explorer above to the “Find a cut-off value” tab, choose the matching region, and enter each percentage to check your table answer.

i. the highest 10%: from Table Z with area = 0.90, z = 1.28.

ii. the highest 60%: from Table Z with area = 0.40, z = -0.25.

iii. the lowest 2%: from Table Z with area = 0.02, z = -2.05.

iv. the lowest 40%: from Table Z with area = 0.40, z = -0.25.

v. the middle 95%: from Table Z with area = 0.025, z = -1.96; with area = 0.975, z = 1.96.

vi. the middle 50%: from Table Z with area = 0.25, z = -0.67; with area = 0.75, z = 0.67.

3.5 Question 2: How do the males in Western Europe stack up?

Based on a Normal model with mean 179 cm and standard deviation of 7 cm describing the heights of males in a particular Western European country:

  1. What proportion of males will be taller than 184 cm?
  2. *Below what height will the shortest 10% of the male population of this country be?
  3. *What percentage of males from this country are between 175 and 185 cm tall?
  4. *Calculate the interquartile range of heights of males from this country.
  5. *Above what height are the tallest 5% of the males in this country?

Use the explorer below to check your calculations. For (a) and (c), use the “Find a proportion” tab. For (b), (d) and (e), use the “Find a cut-off value” tab (note that the IQR in (d) needs the middle 50% cut-offs).

Heights of males: mean = 179 cm, standard deviation = 7 cm
and

(a) z = (184-179)/7 = 0.71. From Table Z, area = 0.7611, so 76.11% are shorter than 184cm. Required area = 1 - 0.7611 = 0.2389, so 23.89% of males will be taller than 184 cm.

(b) From Table Z with area = 0.10, z = -1.28. y = 179 - 1.28 x 7 = 170.04 cm. 10% of the male population will be shorter than 170 cm.

(c) At y=175: z=(175-179)/7=-0.57, area = 0.2843. At y=185: z=(185-179)/7=0.86, area = 0.8051. Required area = 0.8051 - 0.2843 = 0.5208. About 52% of males will be between 175 and 185 cm tall.

(d) From Table Z with area = 0.25, z=-0.67: y = 179 - 0.67 \times 7 = 174.31 cm. With area = 0.75, z=0.67: y = 179 + 0.67 x 7 = 183.69 cm. IQR = 183.69 - 174.31 = 9.38 cm.

(e) From Table Z with area = 0.95, z=1.65: y = 179 + 1.65 \times 7 = 190.55 cm. The tallest 5% of males will be taller than 190.55 cm.

3.6 Question 3: Do they get what they pay for?

Management at a cereal company is concerned that the filling machine at their factory may need some further adjustment to obtain a better balance between the number of satisfied customers (they are getting the amount of cereal that they have paid for based on the advertised volume) and minimizing overfilling (loss of profit). The filling machine currently fills boxes based on a Normal model with mean 720 grams and standard deviation of 30 grams describing the weights of boxes of cereal of a particular variety.

  1. If boxes of this cereal are labelled as containing 700 grams, what proportion of customers are going to be dissatisfied with their purchase?
  2. *Below what weight will the lightest 5% of boxes of this cereal be?
  3. *What percentage of boxes of this cereal is between 700 grams and 750 grams?
  4. *Above what weight are the heaviest 10% of boxes of this cereal?
Cereal box weights: mean = 720 grams, standard deviation = 30 grams
and

(a) z=(700-720)/30=-0.67. From Table Z, area = 0.2514, so 25.14% of customers will receive less than 700 grams.

(b) From Table Z with area = 0.05, z=-1.64. y = 720 - 1.64 \times 30 = 670.8 grams. 5% of boxes will be lighter than 670.8 grams.

(c) At y=700: z=-0.67, area = 0.2514. At y=750: z=1.00, area = 0.8413. Required area = 0.8413 - 0.2514 = 0.5899. About 59% of boxes will be between 700 and 750 grams.

(d) From Table Z with area = 0.90, z=1.28. y = 720 + 1.28 \times 30 = 758.4 grams. 10% of boxes will be heavier than 758.4 grams.

3.7 Question 4: Comparing two groups based on summary statistics

Who should get the prize? Is there much chance that anyone would have scored a better result than the prizewinner?

The local school gives one prize for Social Studies at the end of the school year. Two subjects are studied in the Social Studies discipline - history and geography. George studied history and obtained a result of 86 marks out of 100 overall for the year. Ringo studied geography and obtained a result of 89 marks out of 100 overall for the year.

(a)

Before Speech Night some students in the class thought that Ringo was a certainty to get the Social Studies prize. Explain the students’ reasoning. Do you agree?


At first it would seem reasonable for Ringo to be awarded the prize as he got more marks than George, however it does not take into account how good their marks were compared with the others in their respective classes.

(b)

The Social Studies HOD (Head of Department) studied statistics at university and thought that she had a fair solution. She knew that the distribution of marks in the history class had a mean of 63 with a standard deviation of 12 and the distribution of marks in the geography class had a mean of 60 with a standard deviation of 16. Explain how she could use this information to arrive at a fair solution, and calculate the z-score for each student.

Calculate each student's z-score and check your answer.

(c)

Applying the teacher’s procedure, determine who got the Social Studies Prize.


The HOD could use z-scores to compare each student's result with the rest of the results in their respective classes to see which was more unusual. Because George has the higher positive z-score (1.917 vs 1.813), his result is better than Ringo's, as it is more extreme compared with the results of his classmates than Ringo's is compared with his classmates. George should be awarded the prize.

(d)

What is the probability that anyone would have scored better than the Social Studies prize winner (if the results on the examinations followed a Normal model)?

Try it yourself. Use George’s class context below (mean 63, SD 12 marks) and enter his mark of 86 to find the proportion scoring higher.

History class marks: mean = 63, standard deviation = 12
and

From Table Z with z=1.917, area = 0.9726. Required area = 1 - 0.9726 = 0.0274. There is only a 2.7% chance that anyone would have scored better than George.
