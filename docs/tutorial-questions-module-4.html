4.6 Question 1: Investigate the following research question

Can we predict the energy content of a chocolate bar from the fat content?

SPSS output (Model Summary and Coefficients table) was obtained from the analysis of data from a sample of 16 chocolate bars, relating Fat Content (grams) to Energy (calories).

Model Summary
Model R R Square Adjusted R Square Std. Error of the Estimate
1 .915 .837 .826 74.169
Coefficients
B Std. Error Sig.
(Constant) 1376.859 74.420 .000
Fat Content (grams) 27.245 3.209 .000
  1. Identify the variables, the type of each variable and the units of measure of each variable.
  2. Describe the scatterplot from the SPSS output in terms of form, direction, scatter and outliers.
  3. Do you initially think a linear model is appropriate? Why?
  4. From the SPSS output, what is the correlation coefficient for this data, and explain what it means in this context.
  5. From the SPSS output, what is the value of the slope (\(b_1\)) of the regression line for this data?
  6. From the SPSS output, what is the value of the intercept (\(b_0\)) of the regression line for this data?
  7. Write down the equation of the regression line for predicting Energy from Fat Content (be sure to define the explanatory variable and the response variable in your equation).
  8. I have a chocolate bar with 40 grams of fat, what would be the predicted energy rating of this chocolate bar? (i.e., make a prediction of ‘y’ when x = 40).
  9. Do you think this is a valid prediction? Justify your answer.
  10. *Give an interpretation for what the slope means in this context.
  11. *Give an interpretation of what the intercept means in this context.
  12. *What is \(R^2\), and explain what this means in context.
  13. *Using the residual plot, comment on the suitability of using a linear regression model for this chocolate data.

Check your numbers.

(a) Fat Content: quantitative, measured in grams. Energy: quantitative, measured in calories.

(b) Form - approximately linear. Direction - positive (as Fat Content increases so does the Energy). Scatter - small to slightly moderate. Outliers - none.

(c) Yes, a linear model is appropriate, as Fat Content and Energy are both quantitative variables and the scatterplot indicates the relationship looks to be linear.

(d) The correlation coefficient is r = 0.915. In the Model Summary, R only gives the size of the correlation, not the direction (it is always shown positive). In the Coefficients table, the Standardized Coefficients Beta is equivalent to r and does indicate direction. An r of 0.915 indicates a strong, positive relationship between Fat Content and Energy.

(e) b1 = 27.25.

(f) b0 = 1376.86.

(g) y = 1376.86 + 27.25x, where y = Energy (calories) and x = Fat content (grams).

(h) y = 1376.859 + 27.245(40) = 1376.859 + 1089.8 = 2466.7 calories.

(i) It might be, but you have to be careful when predicting beyond the given data (extrapolation). The relationship may not continue being linear beyond a Fat Content of about 35 grams, and this prediction goes outside the evidence in our sample.

(j) The slope (b1 = 27.25) means that for each 1 gram increase in Fat Content, there is a corresponding increase in Energy of 27.25 calories.

(k) The intercept (b0 = 1376.86) means that if Fat Content is 0 grams, Energy would be 1376.86 calories. In reality this may or may not be the case, as a Fat Content of 0 is outside the range of the data used to produce the equation.

(l) R² = 83.7%. So 83.7% of the variation in Energy is accounted for by the variation in Fat Content; approximately 16% is accounted for by other factors.

(m) The residual plot shows no fanning in or out, and no obvious curvature. The points do not look completely random - more points have a positive residual, and those with a negative residual are more strongly negative. However, with only a small data set, it may still be appropriate to use a linear regression model here.

4.7 Question 2: Investigate the following research question

Does the age of a Corolla have an impact on how much you can ask for it when you sell it?

To answer this research question data was collected on 15 cars. Use the SPSS output below to answer the following questions.

Model Summary
Model R R Square Adjusted R Square Std. Error of the Estimate
1 .972 .944 .940 816.214
Coefficients
B Std. Error Sig.
(Constant) 14285.948 448.673 .000
Age (yr) -959.046 64.581 .000
  1. Identify the variables, the type of variable that each is and the units of measure of each variable.
  2. Describe the scatterplot in terms of form, direction, scatter and outliers.
  3. Do you think a linear model is appropriate? Why?
  4. What is the correlation coefficient for this data, and explain what it means in this context?
  5. From the SPSS output, what is the value of the slope (\(b_1\)) of the regression line for this data?
  6. From the SPSS output, what is the value of the intercept (\(b_0\)) of the regression line for this data?
  7. Write down the equation of the regression line for predicting advertised price from age (be sure to define any variables you use).
  8. I have a Toyota Corolla that is 10 years old, how much do you think I would advertise this for if I were to sell it? (i.e. make a prediction of ‘y’ when x = 10).
  9. *Give an interpretation for what the slope means in this context.
  10. *Give an interpretation for what the intercept means in this context.
  11. *What is \(R^2\) and explain what this means in context.
  12. *From the residual plot of these data in SPSS, comment on the suitability of using a linear regression model for this Corolla data.

Check your numbers.

(a) Age of car: quantitative, measured in years. Price Advertised: quantitative, measured in dollars.

(b) Form - approximately linear. Direction - negative (as Age increases, Advertised Price decreases). Scatter - small. Outliers - none.

(c) Yes, a linear model is appropriate, as Age and Advertised Price are both quantitative variables and the scatterplot indicates the relationship looks to be linear.

(d) The correlation coefficient is r = -0.972. SPSS does not show the negative sign in the Model Summary R value, but it does in the Standardized Coefficients Beta. We also know it should be negative because the scatterplot's direction is negative. An r of -0.972 indicates a strong, negative relationship between Age and Advertised Price.

(e) b1 = -959.05.

(f) b0 = 14285.948.

(g) y = 14285.95 - 959.05x, where y = Advertised Price ($) and x = Age (years).

(h) y = 14285.948 - 959.046(10) = 14285.948 - 9590.46 = 4695.49. The predicted advertised price when Age = 10 years is approximately $4695.

(i) The slope (b1 = -959.05) means that for each 1 year increase in Age, there is a corresponding decrease in Advertised Price of approximately $960.

(j) The intercept (b0 = 14285.948) means that if Age is 0 years, the car would be advertised at approximately $14,286. In reality this may or may not be the case, as an Age of 0 is outside the range of the data used to produce the equation.

(k) R² = 94.4%. So 94.4% of the variation in Advertised Price is accounted for by variation in Age; approximately 6% is accounted for by other factors.

(l) The residual plot shows no fanning in or out, but there does appear to be a slight curved pattern, with positive residuals in the newer and older cars and negative residuals in the medium-aged cars - the points do not look completely random. However, with only a small data set, it may still be appropriate to use a linear regression model here.
